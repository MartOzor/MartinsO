SEO and AI search · Edtech

Getting DigitalWitch's new website found

Problem

DigitalWitch is a real brand, but its new website was starting from nothing. No blog, no sitemap, no way to see how it was doing on Google. The only people finding it were people who already knew the name.

What I did

  • I started by asking what people are really trying to do. Someone typing "best IT training in Nigeria" wants something different from someone typing "how to work from home", so they got different content.
  • Then I built the keyword plan: 450+ keywords across three documents. That is 20 priority keywords, 125 long-tail ones across 10 topics, and 324+ more for local searches, problem searches, AI questions and content clusters.
  • Every keyword got an intent, a competition level, a priority, and a page it belonged to.
  • For people ready to pick a course: "best IT training in Nigeria", "online IT support course Nigeria", "learn IT support without experience".
  • For local searches: "IT Training Lagos", "IT Course Abuja", "Online Tech School Nigeria".
  • For people who have never heard of DigitalWitch: "no experience remote jobs", "how to work from home", "jobs that pay in dollars".
  • For people hunting for a job: "entry level tech jobs", "remote customer support jobs", "virtual assistant jobs".
  • For AI tools: questions like "Can I learn tech without a degree?" and "Which tech skill can I learn in 3 months?", each matched to the page that should answer it.
  • I grouped the articles into four clusters and wrote the easiest wins first: low competition, high intent.
  • I connected Search Console on 18 July 2026, sent Google a 500 page sitemap on 24 July, and checked the numbers as I went.

Technical SEO · Edtech

Fixing DigitalWitch's technical SEO

Problem

Search Console was flagging problems. A copy of the site, the staging version, was being treated as a separate website, and it was quietly taking 11 clicks and 273 impressions from the real one.

What I did

  • I audited the site and wrote up 11 issues, with dates, for the developer.
  • That turned up 87 soft 404s, 30 duplicate pages with no canonical tag, 28 broken pages and 5 server errors.
  • I got SEO fields added to the blog editor: meta title, meta description, slug, author and date.
  • I tested the article schema in Google's Rich Results Test on 2 August 2026.
  • I noted the mobile PageSpeed score so we had something to compare against.
  • I wrote the FAQPage schema code and handed it to the developer.

Result

The blog fields and article schema are live and tested. The rest is with the developers, and it was still waiting when I last checked.

Process

How I work

I do not just sit down and start writing a blog post. There is a lot I check before I write a single word.

  1. Keyword research. What are people searching for? And are those searches relevant to the business?
  2. Search intent. What does someone actually want when they search for that keyword?
  3. The pages already ranking. What are they covering? What are they missing? Can we create something more useful?
  4. The website's existing content. Maybe there is already a page we can improve instead of creating another one.
  5. The technical side. Can Google crawl and index the page properly? Are there technical issues that could affect its visibility?
  6. The business goals. Are we trying to attract potential customers? Build brand awareness? Generate enquiries? Because the goal affects the kind of content we create.

A blog post should not exist just because a business needs to publish something.

What goes into every article

SEO

  • The answer to the main question comes in the first 150 words.
  • Every H2 reads like something a real person would search.
  • 10 FAQs per article, using the exact words people type.
  • Article and FAQPage schema on every post.

Internal links

  • I link each article to other articles on the site, so readers and Google can move around.

AEO, answer engine optimization

  • A direct answer of 40 to 60 words comes right after every H2.
  • Technical terms start with a plain definition.
  • How-to posts are numbered steps.
  • People Also Ask questions become H3 headings with direct answers.
  • Comparisons go in tables.

GEO, generative engine optimization

  • Key facts and salary figures go near the top, never buried.
  • I cite verified outside sources inside the text, so AI tools can pick them up.
  • I write in short, clearly labelled chunks that make sense on their own.
  • Each FAQ answer is complete in 2 to 4 sentences.

Content strategy

  • On a new domain I start with very low and low competition keywords.
  • Every article carries Nigeria specific context.
  • I start with the virtual assistant cluster because it has the widest audience, then build outward.
  • Every week I check Search Console: impressions, clicks, CTR and average position.
